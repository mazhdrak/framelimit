Display: MiniLED Done Right

The SCAR 18's 18-inch MiniLED panel is a highlight of the specification. ASUS lists 2,048 local dimming zones, while published independent measurements report strong HDR brightness and wide DCI-P3 coverage. Brightness, blooming, and color accuracy should be checked against the exact panel mode and test source.

The listed 240Hz refresh rate and selectable color modes suit high-refresh gaming. Response time, HDR behavior, blooming, and color accuracy should be compared using measurements from the exact panel mode rather than inferred from the panel specification.

The one limitation of MiniLED versus OLED is per-pixel contrast. True blacks on OLED are deeper. But for gaming — where bloom from local dimming is more visible than on film — the SCAR 18's MiniLED is arguably preferable. No risk of burn-in, and the extra peak brightness makes HDR content pop in a way that OLED cannot match.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the ASUS ROG Strix SCAR 18 worth it over the Legion Pro 7i?
The Legion Pro 7i uses a lower GPU tier and a smaller display, but value depends on the exact configuration, matched workload evidence, and live checkout price. Choose the SCAR 18 only when its RTX 5090, 18-inch panel, memory capacity, or other verified specifications justify the difference for your workload.
Can the SCAR 18 run at full performance unplugged?
Do not expect the maximum plugged-in GPU power profile on battery. Exact unplugged performance and runtime require a documented matching test; use the supplied adapter for demanding gaming.
Is the MiniLED display better than OLED?
MiniLED prioritizes high peak brightness without OLED burn-in behavior, while OLED provides per-pixel black levels. Compare exact measured brightness, local-dimming behavior, color accuracy, response time, and your static-content usage before choosing.
What is the maximum RAM and storage configuration?
The SCAR 18 supports up to 64GB DDR5-5600 across two SO-DIMM slots. Two M.2 NVMe slots support up to 4TB total storage, configurable in RAID 0 for maximum sequential speeds. Both upgrades are user-accessible without voiding the warranty.
