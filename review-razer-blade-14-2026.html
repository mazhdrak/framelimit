The Blade 14 combines unusually high RTX 5070 power for its size with a sharp OLED panel and compact CNC aluminum chassis. Published results show that native 2880x1800 gaming is demanding, so 1920x1200 or upscaling will often be the practical choice. FRAMELIMIT has not completed a controlled hands-on test of this exact configuration, so no editorial score is assigned.

What the Results Mean

At the panel's native 2880x1800 resolution, the cited Blade 14 averaged 45 FPS in Cyberpunk 2077 and 24 FPS in Black Myth: Wukong at their maximum presets. Dropping to 1920x1200 increased those averages to 95 FPS and 37 FPS respectively. These are native-rendering results, not DLSS or Frame Generation figures, so they provide a useful baseline for deciding when upscaling is needed.

Display, Portability, and Upgrades

The 2880x1800 OLED panel prioritizes sharpness, contrast, and color over esports refresh rate. Its 120Hz ceiling is sufficient for most single-player games, but buyers focused on competitive titles may prefer a 240Hz alternative. At 1.63kg, the Blade 14 is notably portable for an RTX 5070 laptop. Memory is soldered, so the required RAM capacity must be selected at purchase.

Buying Advice

Choose the Blade 14 when portability, build quality, and OLED image quality matter as much as raw FPS. A larger 16-inch laptop will usually provide better performance per dollar and more upgrade flexibility. Amazon listings can mix generations and GPUs, so verify the Ryzen AI 9 365 processor, RTX 5070, 32GB memory, and 120Hz OLED panel before checkout.
