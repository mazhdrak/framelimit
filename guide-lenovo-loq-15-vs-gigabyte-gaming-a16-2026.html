Benchmark Evidence Is Not Equivalent

The LOQ review cites PCWorld’s Ryzen 7 250 and 16GB test configuration, described by that publisher as 115W. Lenovo’s current platform specification lists 100W, so those measurements are not proof of the power limit in every retail offer. The Gigabyte review contains measurements from a related regional 16GB Gaming A16 CVH configuration, while the linked CVHI3US894SH retail SKU has 16GB. Those rows provide useful product-family context but do not establish an exact-SKU FPS winner.

Do not convert the 30W power-limit difference into a fixed performance percentage. Sustained GPU power, firmware, cooling, CPU behavior, memory configuration, rendering mode and game workload all affect the result.

Display, Storage and Portability

The Gigabyte offers the more spacious out-of-box package: 1920×1200 gives extra vertical room, 165Hz is faster than 144Hz, and its 1TB SSD doubles the LOQ's included capacity. Both panels have a published 300-nit specification, so neither should be treated as a bright HDR display.

The Gigabyte's 76Wh battery is larger than the LOQ's 60Wh unit and its listed weight is 0.1kg lower. Capacity and weight are reliable specification differences; runtime still requires a matched brightness, graphics-mode and workload protocol.

Price and Buying Decision

Catalog reference prices are not a live checkout price. Check the exact model code, seller, warranty, memory, storage and final price before ordering. A small price gap may favor the Gigabyte's larger SSD and display; a buyer focused on the best-supported higher-power RTX 5060 configuration may prefer the LOQ.
