Overview — SCAR 18 Performance, 16-inch Size

The ASUS ROG Strix SCAR 16 pairs a maximum 175W RTX 5080 specification with ASUS's liquid-metal cooling design and MiniLED display technology in a smaller chassis than the SCAR 18. Its 2.8kg weight still places it firmly in the desktop-replacement class.

The 175W maximum power specification matches several competing RTX 5080 laptops, but sustained performance cannot be inferred from TGP alone. Cooling, firmware, CPU load, memory, and selected mode all affect the delivered result.

Compare the live checkout price with the Legion Pro 7i, Razer Blade 16, and other RTX 5080 systems. MiniLED versus OLED, portability, memory serviceability, and matching workload evidence are more useful decision factors than an unsupported universal performance or value claim.

Display: MiniLED at 16 Inches

ASUS describes the 16-inch panel as a matte Mini LED "Nebula HDR" display with over 2,000 dimming zones, 1,200 nits peak brightness, 100% DCI-P3, 240Hz and 3ms response. Ultrabookreview identifies a BOE panel and lists 2,304 zones, 1,100 nits HDR and 500 nits SDR, but its measurement setup did not work on this laptop, so no independent brightness, contrast or colour-accuracy figures are available from that review.

The panel can run as a single-zone display with IPS-like uniformity or with multi-zone local dimming for games and video. It is one of the few 2025 high-performance 16-inch laptops with a matte Mini LED rather than a glossy OLED: no burn-in concerns, but less perfect black levels. Both SCAR models list over 2,000 dimming zones; at 16 inches the 2560×1600 panel has about 189 PPI versus about 168 on the 18-inch model.

In Performance mode the GPU ran at 135–150W and delivered roughly 90–95% of Turbo-mode performance with quieter fans. Silent mode caps the GPU at 55W. Ultrabookreview notes that the plastic armrest stays cooler to the touch under load than a metal one would.

Battery Life

The battery is 90Wh. At about 120 nits and 60Hz, Ultrabookreview measured roughly 3–5 hours of light use depending on the task and RGB lighting, and about 1 hour of gaming (Witcher 3, Performance mode). The laptop also charges over USB-C at up to 100W.

Build, Keyboard, and Ports

Ultrabookreview describes a plastic main chassis with a metal lid, a sharp front lip, and an excellent, quiet keyboard. A latch releases the bottom panel without tools, giving access to both SO-DIMM slots, two M.2 2280 slots (PCIe 5.0 supported), the fans and the battery. There is no card reader. All exhausts are at the back.

Use the exact ASUS specification and retailer listing to verify keyboard layout, memory and storage access, card reader, USB, display output, networking, and webcam configuration because regional variants can differ.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the SCAR 16 worth it over the Legion Pro 7i Gen 10?
Both expose a 175W RTX 5080 specification, but that does not guarantee identical sustained performance. The SCAR 16 uses MiniLED while the Legion Pro 7i uses OLED. Compare the exact display configuration, weight, serviceability, matched benchmark rows, and live checkout price before deciding.
Can the SCAR 16 run 4K gaming?
It can drive an external 4K display through its supported display output, but playable settings depend on the game, ray tracing, upscaling mode, and power profile. Use exact matching 4K benchmark evidence rather than a generic FPS range.
Does the SCAR 16 have a MUX switch?
Yes. ASUS includes a hardware MUX switch accessible in Armoury Crate. Enabling it bypasses the iGPU and routes display output directly through the RTX 5080, which usually improves gaming frame rates; Advanced Optimus can switch automatically. The gain varies by game and is not quantified here.
What RAM does the SCAR 16 support and can I upgrade?
Two SO-DIMM DDR5-5600 slots support up to 64GB. The base 32GB (2x16GB) is sufficient for gaming. For heavy content creation workloads running simultaneously with games, 64GB is worth the upgrade. Access is tool-less via the bottom panel.
